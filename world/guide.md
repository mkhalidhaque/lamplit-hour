# Fernwick guide (Fernwick Nights)

Fernwick is a small, quiet seaside town where nothing much happens. Every story is another evening there. Everything is invented.

## Ground rules
- No villains, danger, illness, death, loud events or hurry. Weather is mild or cozy; storms stay distant.
- No organized religion: no worship, clergy, places of worship, scripture or religious holidays. Festivals are secular.
- Seasons follow the real calendar on the day the episode goes up.
- Gentle, old technology: lamps, kettles, ferries, bicycles, trains, radios. No phones or screens.
- Every episode opens "Welcome back to Fernwick." and ends "Good night." The rest is pure narration.

## Recurring cast (gentle, kind, never in a hurry; they appear lightly so any episode stands alone)
- Oriel Penhallow: keeper of the Salt Lamp, mid-60s, patient and dry-humored. Polishes the brass lens in slow circles and hums off-key.
- Tomas Marrow: night baker, large and soft-spoken. Talks to the dough; keeps a radio low on the shelf.
- Wilhelmina "Willa" Quince: owner of Pennywhistle Books. Never remembers where she shelved anything, always finds it.
- Barnaby Teague: ferry pilot, quiet and weathered. Counts the waves between harbor and island in a murmur.
- Ines Larkspur: tea-room owner. Rearranges the same six cups every evening.
- Pip: cat of uncertain ownership. Sleeps on the warmest thing in each story.
- Mr. Aldous Reed: stationmaster, retired but never off duty. Winds the station clock at the same time each night.

## Seasons and secular festivals
- Spring: pale gold evenings, fresh rain, open windows. Blossom Walk (lamps along the lane).
- Summer: long blue dusk, warm stone, moths at the lamp. Midsummer Lanterns on the harbor.
- Autumn: amber and low, leaf smoke, woolen blankets. Harvest Fair on the green.
- Winter: early dark, frost, steam on windows. First Snow Supper in the tea room.

## Series
Fernwick Evenings, Night Journeys, Keepers of the Night, Weather Watch, Slow Craft, Quiet Wilds, Old Fernwick (the same places a century ago), Myths Retold Slowly (public-domain myths and folklore retold gently as stories, never as belief, no sacred figures of living faiths, no battle or danger).

## Visual style
Soft gouache-like scenes, deep shadow, one warm light source. People small or from behind, never faces to camera. Deep navy, dark teal, charcoal with amber and cream light.

## Details added by stories
- Bakery (Oct 7): the sourdough starter lives in a crock by the window; Tomas keeps a small radio turned very low.
- Green Kettle (Oct 8): Ines knits in an old basket with a lid that never shuts, from a pattern card found in a biscuit tin; she secretly knits Barnaby Teague a pair of mittens every autumn and leaves them on the ferry wheel in brown paper tied with red wool, no note (since his first cold night in the tea room, 22 years ago). Barnaby thinks Oriel knits them; Oriel cannot knit. Ines dries orange peel on the stove; her six cups are blue, white, white, green, white, and one with a chipped gold rim. The clock over the counter always says quarter to nine. The lamplighter rides a bicycle along Harbor Lane.
