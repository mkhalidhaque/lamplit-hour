# Lesser-known Greek myth ideas (Fernwick Nights, from 2026-10-15)

A starter list for the daily story. Pick freely, add new ideas, and write "USED <date>" after a figure once told
(world/greek_figures.csv is the real record; qc.py checks it). "Care" notes say which part to soften or skip.
Check every detail against the ancient sources before writing; these notes are reminders, not the source.

## Night, sleep and dreams (good any night; best for 17 to 31 October)
- **Hypnos** (Sleep): lives in a cave where the sun never reaches, poppies at the door, the river Lethe murmuring past, no rooster, no dog, no creaking gate (Ovid, Metamorphoses 11). In the Iliad (14) Hera talks him into putting Zeus to sleep; he agrees only after she promises him one of the Graces, Pasithea, for a wife. Gentle humor.
- **Morpheus and the Oneiroi**: the sons of Sleep who carry dreams; Morpheus can copy any human face and voice (Ovid, Met. 11). Dreams come through two gates, one of horn (true) and one of ivory (false) (Odyssey 19).
- **Nyx** (Night): so old and respected that even Zeus did not want to upset her (Iliad 14). Her house at the edge of the world, where she and her daughter Hemera (Day) greet each other on the bronze threshold as one goes out and the other comes in (Hesiod, Theogony 744-757).
- **Hemera** (Day), see Nyx. A lovely two-sided story of mother and daughter who never share the house.
- **Lethe**: the river of forgetting in the underworld; its water brings rest. Told as landscape, not as afterlife belief.
- **Asteria**: Titan goddess of falling stars; she leapt into the sea as a quail and became a floating island, later Delos, where Leto finally found a place to rest (Hesiod, Theogony 409; Apollodorus 1.4.1; Callimachus, Hymn to Delos). Care: she was fleeing Zeus; say only that she did not wish to be found.

## Sea and wind
- **Alcyone and Ceyx** (USED 2026-10-15): a king lost at sea, a dream sent through Morpheus, and both turned into kingfishers; Aeolus calms the sea for seven days each winter while they nest, the "halcyon days" (Ovid, Met. 11; Apollodorus 1.7.4). Care: the shipwreck is one soft sentence.
- **Glaucus**: a fisherman who sees his catch revive on a strange grass, tastes it and becomes a sea god with a green-blue beard (Ovid, Met. 13).
- **Proteus**: the Old Man of the Sea who herds seals off Pharos and changes into a lion, a tree, running water to avoid answering questions (Odyssey 4). Humorous.
- **Ino / Leucothea**: the sea goddess who rises like a gull and gives the shipwrecked Odysseus her veil to float him to shore (Odyssey 5). Care: skip her earlier life.
- **Aeolus**: keeper of the winds on a floating island with a wall of bronze; gives Odysseus the winds tied in an ox-hide bag (Odyssey 10).
- **Arion**: the singer carried to shore on a dolphin's back; the dolphin became a constellation (Herodotus 1.23-24; Hyginus). Care: the sailors' threat is brief and soft.
- **Arethusa**: a nymph who became a spring and flows under the sea from Greece to rise on the island of Ortygia in Sicily (Ovid, Met. 5). Care: she was pursued; keep it gentle and short.

## Stars and sky
- **Astraea**: the star maiden who was the last of the immortals to leave the earth and became the constellation Virgo (Aratus, Phaenomena 96-136; Ovid, Met. 1).
- **The Pleiades**: seven sisters, daughters of Atlas; one star is dim because Merope married a mortal (Hyginus, Astronomica 2.21; Ovid, Fasti 4).
- **The Hyades**: the rain stars, who cared for the infant Dionysus; their rising brought the rains (Hyginus; Ovid, Fasti 5).
- **Kedalion**: the apprentice who rode on giant Orion's shoulders to guide him east to the sunrise (Apollodorus 1.4.3). Care: Orion's blindness is one soft line.
- **Hesperus**: the evening star, brother of the morning star.
- **Iris**: the rainbow messenger who carries the water of the Styx in a golden jug (Hesiod, Theogony 775-806). Also visits the cave of Hypnos (Ovid, Met. 11).
- **Tithonus**: the Dawn asked Zeus to make her husband live forever and forgot to ask that he stay young; in later tellings he becomes a cicada (Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite 218-238).

## Earth, gardens and craft
- **The Hesperides**: daughters of evening who tend the golden apples in a garden at the western edge of the world, with Atlas holding up the sky nearby (Hesiod, Theogony 215; Apollodorus 2.5.11). Care: the serpent Ladon is a sleepy guardian, never a fight.
- **Clytie**: the nymph who watched the sun cross the sky for nine days until she became a flower that turns to follow it (Ovid, Met. 4). Care: skip or soften what happened to Leucothoe.
- **Philemon and Baucis**: a poor old couple who welcome two strangers (Zeus and Hermes); the wine jug keeps refilling; they ask never to be parted and become an oak and a linden side by side (Ovid, Met. 8). Care: leave out the flood of the neighbors' valley or tell it as a lake appearing; their wish is told without temples.
- **Amalthea**: the goat who fed the infant Zeus in a cave on Crete; one horn became the horn of plenty, and she became the star Capella (Hyginus; Ovid, Fasti 5).
- **Melissa**: the nymph who fed baby Zeus honey and gave her name to bees.
- **Aristaeus**: the shepherd-beekeeper who lost his bees and had to catch Proteus to learn why (Virgil, Georgics 4; Apollodorus).
- **Perdix**: Daedalus' young nephew who invented the saw from a fish's spine and the compass for drawing circles, and became the partridge that flies low (Ovid, Met. 8). Care: the fall is one gentle sentence.
- **The Heliades**: the sisters of Phaethon who became poplars on the river Eridanus, their tears hardening into amber (Ovid, Met. 2). Care: Phaethon's ride is told softly, at a distance.
- **Cyparissus**: a boy and his tame stag with golden antlers; grief turned him into the cypress (Ovid, Met. 10). Sad but tender.
- **Elpis** (Hope): the one thing left in the jar after everything else flew out (Hesiod, Works and Days 90-105).
- **Kairos**: the spirit of the right moment, with a lock of hair in front and bald behind, so you can only catch him as he comes (later Greek sources, statue by Lysippos). Short; pair with a story.
- **Philoctetes**: the archer who lit the pyre of Heracles, received his bow, was left alone for ten years on Lemnos, and was needed at Troy in the end (Sophocles, Philoctetes; Apollodorus Epitome 3, 5). Care: the wound and abandonment are told softly; focus on his years with the island, the bow and the return.
- **Talos**: the bronze giant who walked around Crete three times a day (Apollonius, Argonautica 4). Care: skip or soften his end.
