# Daily story task (read fully, then do it)

You write one new sleep story for the YouTube channel Fernwick Nights and publish it by committing a JSON file. Everything is original; nothing here is religious.

## Steps
1. Read INSTRUCTIONS.md (the channel rules on one page), then world/guide.md and world/story_log.csv, and look at the last ~14 rows. Also skim the recent files in published/ so you do not repeat a premise, task, opening image or ending image.
2. Choose tonight's story yourself. Khalid's direction (2026-10-07): lean on lesser-known stories from around the world that English-speaking listeners are unlikely to know. Rotate regions (check the source_tradition column of the log and do not repeat a region within 7 days) and keep it copyright-safe (see rule 9). Fernwick stories can borrow a small motif, craft or custom from a tradition; Myths Retold Slowly retells a whole tale. Rotate series (do not use the same series twice in a row; use Myths Retold Slowly about once a week). Pick a place and a recurring character (or none) that were not used in the last 14 days together. Use the real season of the date you are running on, a matching weather and an ambience bed (rain, wind, ocean, harbor or stove).
3. Write the story as described in the rules below.
4. Save it as queue/YYYY-MM-DD.json for the date you are running on (UTC date is fine).
5. Run `python scripts/qc.py queue/YYYY-MM-DD.json`. If it fails, fix the story and run it again until it passes (up to 3 tries; if it still fails, do not commit; stop and explain).
6. Add any new places, people or details you invented to world/guide.md (short bullet under the right heading), and commit everything to the main branch with the message `Story YYYY-MM-DD: <title>`. Pushing the file in queue/ starts the video build and a private YouTube upload automatically. Do not change anything else.

## Rules
1. Entirely original telling. No existing characters, books, films, songs, brands or real people. Invented place names only. Exception: the Myths Retold Slowly series may retell public-domain myths and folklore (Greek, Norse, Celtic, and similar) in an original, quiet way, as stories and never as scripture or living-faith content.
2. Almost nothing happens. One small task or journey, done step by step. No villains, danger, injury, illness, death, arguments, deadlines, loud sounds, sudden events, or anything frightening, even mildly. No organized-religion content (no prayer, worship, clergy, places of worship, scripture, saints, religious holidays or symbols). Festivals stay secular.
3. Energy only goes down. Sentences get a little shorter and softer as the story goes on. The character settles to sleep at the end.
4. Concrete and specific, every scene (Khalid, 2026-10-08): each scene gives the listener a sound, a smell (or taste), a texture and a temperature, plus precise sights (colors, shapes, light). Name things exactly: the six cups are blue, white, white, green, white and one with a chipped gold rim, not "some cups".
5. Write like a human storyteller: plain words, varied sentence openings, occasional gentle humor. Do not use: tapestry, symphony, embrace, testament, nestled, whisper of, a sense of, as if the world itself, little did, in the heart of, bustling, delve, journey of, magical.
6. A storyteller tells every story (Khalid, 2026-10-08). Each night a different wise teller tells the story to someone: a lighthouse keeper, an old ferryman, a grandmother by the stove, a retired stationmaster, a traveling clockmender, a beekeeper, a ship's cook. The teller can be a Fernwick regular from the guide or someone new and passing through; rotate them (no teller twice within 7 days, check the teller column of world/story_log.csv). The teller speaks in the first person to the listener, gently addressing them as "you" ("Come and sit by the stove. I will tell you who leaves them."), a few short asides in their own voice (what they saw, what they think, a dry little joke), while the scenes themselves are told in plain present-tense narration. The teller knows the story because they saw it, heard it, or were part of it. No breathing or relaxation instructions and no "sleep well" in the story itself. At most three short lines of dialogue from the characters.
7. Avoid words the checker blocks as alarming or religious even in passing (for example danger, dead*, gun*, attack, panic, scream, blood, crash, monster, knife, died, suddenly, holy, angel, pray*, church, saint, bible).
9. World stories, copyright-safe. Use only public-domain or oral-tradition folklore as inspiration (old tales, proverbs, traditional crafts and customs). Write an original retelling in the Fernwick voice; never follow the plot beats, wording or structure of one specific modern translation, anthology or retelling, since those can be copyrighted. Skip sacred or ceremonial stories of living cultures and anything from religious texts or practices; ordinary folk tales, animal tales and craft or weather lore are fine. Fill source_tradition (people/region) and source_note (one sentence: the tale or motif is traditional/public domain and the telling is original).
10. Cozy-spooky season (Khalid, 2026-10-07): from 17 to 31 October, about every other day may be a "cozy spooky" Halloween-season story (lantern-lit autumn nights, friendly harmless ghosts or house spirits, creaky stairs that are only the house settling, pumpkins, mist, owls, secular harvest and costume fun). It must still be a sleep story: nothing truly frightening, no villain, no threat, no jump or loud sound, and everything turns out to be gentle and explained. Keep every rule above. Note that the checker blocks words like "shadowy figure", "scream", "blood" and "dead*"; do not work around them. Use series Old Fernwick or Weather Watch, and say "Halloween" only in tags and the description, not as a religious holiday.
11. Long stories become parts (Khalid, 2026-10-07): if a story cannot finish inside one video (about 2,000 words), split it into parts on consecutive days. Put "(Part 1 of 3)" in story_title and in every title option, decide the number of parts up front and record the plan in new_world_facts, and write each part as a complete, calm story that ends settled for sleep (never a cliffhanger). Open parts 2 and later with one soft sentence recalling the last part. Check world/story_log.csv and published/ each day for an unfinished multi-part story and continue it before starting something new.
8. Mark pauses with [pause] (2 s) and [long pause] (4 s) on their own lines between paragraphs, more toward the end.

## Structure of the script (about 1,750 to 2,150 words in total; at ~110 words a minute that is 16 to 20 minutes)
Khalid (2026-10-08): stories must open with a hook that keeps listeners with us before they drift off, give real context, and be rich in description and substance. A pleasant mood with nothing behind it is not enough. Each story has a clear arc that eases down over its last third.

Chapters: put `[[CHAPTER Name]]` on its own line where each part begins (they become YouTube chapters). The script starts with `[[CHAPTER Welcome]]`, the first telling has 4 to 6 chapters with short, gentle names (The Ferry Wheel, How It Began, Stars and Pine), and the second telling starts with `[[CHAPTER Once More, Slowly]]`. The build adds a final "Quiet ambience to stay asleep" chapter by itself.

A. Intro (one paragraph, about 40 words): starts exactly "Welcome to Fernwick Nights." Then says tonight's story title and the teller ("Tonight's story is The Mitten with the Small Stars, told by Oriel Penhallow, who keeps the lighthouse at the north point.") and ends with one soft "get comfortable" line. Then `[long pause]`.
B. Hook (about 100 to 150 words): the teller greets the listener in a line or two (where they are sitting together), then the hook: a small, gentle question or mystery the story will answer (for example: every autumn someone leaves mittens on the ferry wheel and the pilot has never learned who). Never open with the date or the weather; never a cliffhanger or anything tense. Answer the question by the middle of the story at the latest.
C. Context (about 100 to 150 words): who the character is, where Fernwick and tonight's place are, the season, and why tonight's small task matters to someone. A new listener should understand the world after these paragraphs.
D. Backstory and substance (2 or 3 paragraphs, woven in): how this came to be (a remembered evening, a person it is for, where the object or custom came from, how the teller knows). For world-tradition stories, a few sentences of real, accurate background about the craft, custom or tale and the region it comes from, told as storytelling, not a lecture.
E. The story (about 1,000 to 1,200 words in 16 to 22 short paragraphs): the task done step by step, rich in sensory detail (rule 4), with one or two moments of gentle humor or character. The last third slows: shorter sentences, more [long pause] markers, the character settling to sleep.
F. A line `[[SECOND]]` on its own, then `[[CHAPTER Once More, Slowly]]` and the second telling (about 120 to 200 words): the same story retold more simply and slowly, opening with a one-line echo of the hook.
G. Outro (one or two sentences from the teller, then a `[long pause]`): a soft goodbye that hands the listener over to the quiet ("And that is the story of the mittens. The kettle is still warm, and you can stay by the stove as long as you like."). Final line exactly: "Good night." The build then plays a few minutes of ambience alone.

Voice: pick the voice that suits tonight's teller from this list (all free edge-tts voices) and put it in the "voice" field: en-US-AvaNeural (warm, soft female), en-US-AndrewNeural (warm, low male), en-GB-SoniaNeural (calm, dry female, British), en-GB-RyanNeural (gentle male, British), en-US-EmmaNeural (bright, kind female), en-US-BrianNeural (relaxed male), en-IE-EmilyNeural (soft female, Irish), en-IE-ConnorNeural (soft male, Irish). Prefer Ava or Andrew unless the teller clearly calls for another.

Before saving, reread the first two paragraphs and ask: would a stranger want to hear what happens next, and do they know who, where and why? If not, rewrite them.
